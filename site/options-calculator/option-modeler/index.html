    Key terms
    • Option: a contract giving the buyer a right, not a duty, to buy (a call) or sell (a put) shares at a set price by a set date.
    • Call: the right, but not the duty, to buy shares at the strike price by the expiry date.
    • Put: the right, but not the duty, to sell shares at the strike price by the expiry date.
    • Long: bought. The buyer pays the premium. Short: sold, also called written. The seller receives the premium and takes on the duty if the buyer uses the right.
    • Strike: the set price in the contract. Premium: the price of the option, quoted per share. Expiry: the last day the option exists.
    • Contract: a standard contract is usually for 100 shares. For example, a made-up premium of $3.00 per share is $300.00 for one standard contract.
    • In the money: a call with the share price above its strike, or a put with the share price below. At the money: the share price equals the strike. Out of the money: the reverse of in the money. In the money is not the same as a profit: the premium paid comes off.
    • Break-even: the price at expiry where the position's profit or loss is exactly $0.00.
    • Net debit and net credit: the money paid, or received, in total when all the legs are opened.
    • Payoff at expiry: what the options pay, or cost, on the last day, from the price on that day.
    More about this tool

    About this tool. The option modeler works out what a position typed by you would pay at expiry. It uses only the numbers you type and the simplifications listed under "What can go wrong". It does not know what any option costs, whether a price will rise or fall, or which positions are available in any market. The calculation runs in your browser; the numbers you type are not stored, saved, logged or sent anywhere by this tool. The results are illustrations of arithmetic. They do not show what any real option has done or would do. Options can lose all the money paid for them, and selling an option can lose far more than the money received for it. A result is arithmetic on the numbers typed and may not match a real position. We do not give financial advice, and nothing on this page is advice to buy, sell or use any option. This tool is also not investment advice, tax advice or legal advice. This tool sells nothing. Apart from other pages of this site and our own app, it links to nothing.
