Short call (also called writing a call)

What it is

One call is sold, and the premium is received. At expiry the seller pays the price above the strike, per share.

Worked example

Options can lose all the money paid for them. Selling an option can lose far more than the money received for it, and some positions have no limit on the loss. Nothing here is advice to buy, sell or use any option.

For education only, not financial advice.

Every price, strike and premium here is a made-up example until you type your own. A profit or loss here is arithmetic on the numbers shown. It does not show what any real option has done or would do.

Example: strike $50.00, premium $3.00, price at expiry $58.00 gives a loss of $500.00 per contract; it breaks even at $53.00 per share.

Maximum profit (example)
$300.00, for any price from $0.00 to $50.00
Maximum loss (example)
No limit: the loss grows $100.00 for each $1.00 the price at expiry is above $50.00 per share.
Break-even price (example)
$53.00 per share
Net credit received (example)
$3.00 per share

For education only, not financial advice.

What can go wrong

The loss has no limit, because a price has no top. The seller of a call can lose far more than the $300.00 received.

The seller of an option can lose far more than the money received for it. The seller of a call has no limit on the loss. The seller of a put can lose up to the strike less the premium, for each share.
